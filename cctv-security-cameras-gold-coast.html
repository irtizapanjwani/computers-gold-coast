Wired vs. Wireless

Reasons To Stay Away From DIY Wireless Cameras.

Brands like Arlo, Ring, Google Nest, Blink and TP-Link Tapo all offer tempting DIY kits — here's why a professional hard-wired install still wins.

📶

Reliability

A stable, continuous connection — no signal drops or interference that can affect wireless systems.

🔒

Security

Wired systems are far less vulnerable to hacking than wireless cameras transmitting data over the air.

🎞️

Video Quality

Higher resolution, clearer feeds — not limited by bandwidth or WiFi signal strength.

🔋

No Batteries Required

Powered directly — no recharging, and no risk of a flat camera exactly when an incident happens.

💵

Long-Term Value

More durable and lower maintenance over time — no ongoing subscription fees for notifications or mobile access.

📈

Scalability

Easy to expand with more cameras, without straining your home WiFi network.

Frequently Asked Questions

CCTV Camera Questions, Answered.

What's the difference between a licensed master security installer and someone who isn't?

We're not only licensed and qualified to install security cameras, but a master cabler as well — meaning your cabling is neat, tidy and follows industry standards, to the level of a fully experienced data cable installer. Common beginner mistakes we've seen at other jobs include:

  • Poor camera placement
  • Neglecting sufficient lighting
  • Failure to secure cameras properly
  • Messy cabling done by someone unqualified
  • Failing to consider privacy concerns
What are the different types of CCTV cameras?

With so many options, here's a brief overview of what we work with:

  • IP / Network Camera — almost all cameras now, letting you check footage and live feed from anywhere in the world.
  • Turret Camera — small, rounded, dome-shaped cameras pointing to one side with a wide capture angle. Common in homes and businesses.
  • Bullet Camera — the long-headed camera style seen in shops and malls, great for zooming into specific areas like number plates.
  • Dome Camera — dome-shaped, mostly indoor/commercial, hides which way the lens is pointing.
Can your CCTV record sound?

Yes — all of our systems can record sound, and some have 2-way audio so you can speak to whoever triggers the alarm, for example giving a delivery driver permission to leave a package somewhere specific.

Can CCTV footage be used as evidence?

Yes. CCTV footage is a crucial tool in legal and law enforcement scenarios, used for prevention, detection, investigation, prosecution or punishment of offences, and in court or tribunal proceedings. It must be handled correctly — clear, unaltered, and timestamped — to serve as reliable evidence.

Will installing CCTV bring down my insurance premium?

Many insurers do offer lower premiums for properties with a monitored security system installed — it's worth asking your insurer directly, as policies vary. We can provide documentation of your installed system if your insurer requires it.
